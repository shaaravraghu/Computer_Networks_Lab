Reason for Congestion:
1. Excessive Traffic Load (exceeds link capacity)
2. Bursty Traffic Patterns (sudden bursts can overwhelm buffers)
3. Insufficient Buffering/ Slow Processing at Routing/ Switches
4. Re-Transmission Overhead (consecutive messages in a system with duplicate messages and failing (to-be resent) messages)
5. Bottleneck Links (links in between with less broadband wrt rest of network)
6. Unfair Bandwidth Sharing with multiple connections
7. Competition for Resources

Consequences of Congestion:
1. Delayed Response (Sometimes Duplicate Send ocuurs because of acknowledgement failure)
2. Packet Loss 
3. Reduced Throughput
4. Poor Goodput (important necessary info)

Flow Control v/s Congestion Control:
- Prevents receiver buffer from being overwhelmed | network (routers & links) from being overwhelmed
- point-to-point problem | end-to-end network wide problem
- receiver window (rwnd) - advertised by receiver | congestion window (cwnd) - maintained at sender
- data link and transport layer | network and transport layer
- sliding window protocol | TCP, Tahoe, CUBIC, BBR, Reno
- at max prevents one sender | can prevent multiple senders

Reasons for Retransmission:
1. Packet Loss
2. Acknowledgement Loss lead to duplicates (slow arrival of acknowledgement/ acknowledgement failure)

Approach towards congestion control:
1. End-to-end Congestion Control: congestion inferred from observed delays/ loss
2. Network Assisted Congestion Control: direct feedback to sender and receiver

Sender Window = min(CWND, RWND)
