– Robust (towards failures or errors)

Advantages of Layering:
Modular design – Explicit structures allows identification, relationship of complex systems
Software reuse – Upper layers can share lower layers functionality (HTTP and SMTP can be use TCP)
Abstraction of Implementation – Can change specific parts of implementation as long as interface kept same

Network Topologies:
1. Bus Topology – All devices are connected to a single shared backbone cable. Data travels along the cable, making it simple and inexpensive but a backbone failure can affect the entire network.
2. Star Topology – All devices are connected to a central hub/switch. It is easy to manage and troubleshoot, but failure of the central device can bring down the network.
3. Ring Topology – Each device is connected to two neighboring devices, forming a closed loop. Data travels around the ring, but failure of a link/device can disrupt the network.
4. Mesh Topology – Devices are connected to many or all other devices, providing multiple paths for data. It is highly reliable but expensive and complex to install.
5. Hybrid Topology – Combines two or more different topologies, such as star-bus or star-ring. It offers flexibility and scalability but is more complex and costly.

Categories of Network:
LAN – Local Area Network covering a small area like a room, building, or campus.
MAN – Metropolitan Area Network covering a city or large metropolitan area.
WAN – Wide Area Network covering large geographical areas such as countries or continents.

Protocols - set of rules that govern data communication (syntax: structure/ format/ order of data, semantics: meaning of each section, timing)
OSI (Open Systems Interconnection) - defined by ISO (International Standardisation Organisation)

OSI MODEL – 7 LAYERS
7. Application   – Provides network services directly to end-user applications.
6. Presentation  – Handles data translation, encryption/decryption, and compression.
5. Session       – Establishes, manages, and terminates communication sessions.
4. Transport     – Provides end-to-end delivery, reliability, flow control, and segmentation.
3. Network       – Handles logical addressing and routing of packets between networks.
2. Data Link     – Provides node-to-node/ hop-to-hop (linked list) delivery, framing, MAC addressing, and error detection.
1. Physical      – Transmits raw bits over the physical communication medium.
